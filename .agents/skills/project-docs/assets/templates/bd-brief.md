## What this makes possible

The new capability in the words a customer would use. Not features — what
they can now do, or stop doing.

## Who it matters to

The segments or accounts this changes the answer for. If it changes nothing
for anyone outside the company, there should be no commercial brief at all.

## What it does NOT do

The boundary, stated plainly. **This is the most important section here.**
The failure mode of commercial material is a promise nobody can keep, and
it is almost always made in the gap between what shipped and what someone
assumed shipped.

## Availability

When, and how firm that is. Distinguish "committed", "planned", and
"exploratory" explicitly — a reader will otherwise assume the strongest one.

## Where this leaves us

What it unlocks, what it answers that we previously could not, and what it
does not change. Comparisons to alternatives belong here only if they are
verifiable.

## Still open

Anything unresolved that a customer conversation might run into. Better
surfaced here than discovered in a meeting.
